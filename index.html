The Welfare Gate: Pre-Fisc Inequality, Universal Public Services, and Democratic Resilience in Post-1990 Democratizers

The democratic-erosion literature is split. One camp finds that income inequality is among the strongest predictors of democratic decline in a global sample of uninterrupted democracies [1]. The other, reading the post-1990 wave case by case, finds that economic grievances did not systematically separate backsliders from survivors [2]. This paper argues the dispute is unfalsifiable as both sides run it: the statistical camp tests no conditioning structure, and its flagship treatment, disposable-income inequality, is post-fisc, compressed by exactly the welfare state the gate hypothesis implicates. The registered design corrects both problems. Market-inequality surges, measured pre-fisc, erode the V-Dem Liberal Democracy Index three to five years later only where universal public health and education services built in the first democratic decade are thin. The number that frames the test: the gate holds only if the inequality-by-welfare interaction is significant at p < 0.05 in the primary specification and in at least five of the seven robustness cells. No model has been estimated. The estimates do not exist yet; this page is the design.

01Contributions

Four claims, each registered before any estimation.

Reconciliation on institutional grounds

Neither leading account tests the conditioning structure. This design identifies the condition under which each holds: inequality erodes democracy where welfare institutions are thin, and the coalitional story dominates where they are thick. One interaction test, not two literatures, adjudicates the dispute.

A treatment the institutions cannot compress

Disposable-income inequality is post-fisc, and fiscal systems that compress it are larger where welfare states are larger, so a gate estimated on the disposable Gini cannot separate institutional transmission from arithmetic filtering. The treatment moves pre-fisc, to the WID pre-tax top-10 percent income share, and the market-minus-disposable Gini gap is measured directly, turning the entanglement into a test of the stabilization mechanism.

The democratic dividend, conditioned

Democratization raises tax revenues and schooling but shows no robust average effect on inequality. The design states the dividend on top-income shares over a fixed post-transition window, years 0 to 7, and links its size to regime resilience in 2010–2022: the inequality-reduction leg materializes only in high-welfare-legacy countries.

Identification and power fixed before the results

The gate is identified off deviations from welfare-specific trends, with estimators built for differential treatment timing, a fixed roster with a documented coverage matrix, exogeneity probes, and a simulation-based minimum-detectable-effect analysis. The confirmation criteria are calibrated to the power the sample can actually deliver.

02Method

The dispute neither side can resolve

Rau and Stokes estimate a main effect: in a sample of democracies uninterrupted over 1995–2020, the Gini coefficient is among the strongest predictors of erosion onset, with 23 spells of backsliding in 22 countries. Haggard and Kaufman compare backsliders with survivors and conclude that economic grievances did not do the separating; polarization and elite coalitional choices did. The statistical account contains no welfare variables, so it cannot see whether its inequality coefficient is carried by thin-welfare democracies. The case-based account never formalizes the interaction a conditional claim requires. Both miss the same object, and the field's flagship treatment is entangled with it: disposable income is post-fisc, and the fiscal systems that compress it are the welfare states this hypothesis implicates.

What goes in

  • Sample. Post-1990 democratizers with transitions beginning 1989–1998, an expected roster of 25 to 35 countries over a panel running 1985–2023. The cohort cap makes pre-determination possible: every retained case's first democratic decade ends before the 2008 crisis and before the 2010–2022 resilience window. Transition is dated from the V-Dem Regimes of the World series and cross-checked against Boix-Miller-Rosato; the roster is fixed deterministically, and no case is added or dropped after estimation begins.
  • Treatment. The primary treatment is the World Inequality Database pre-tax national-income top-10 percent income share, the measure type Rau and Stokes use at the top end of their own analysis. It is lagged three years in the main specification, with distributed lags tracing years one through six, implementing the three-to-five-year transmission window. The market-income Gini is the secondary treatment; the disposable Gini, where the entanglement lives, enters only to construct the gap.
  • Gate. The welfare variable W is the sum of government health expenditure and government education expenditure, both as a share of GDP, averaged over the earliest available three-year window within years 0 to 8 after transition and held time-invariant. It is deliberately non-cash: health and education are what the broad middle class uses, what is automatic in delivery, and what an eroding executive must capture. Pensions and targeted cash are excluded by construction, and they become the placebo moderators of H5.
  • Outcome. The V-Dem Liberal Democracy Index, 0 to 1, in levels and in five-year differences. A continuous index is essential: the hypothesis concerns graded erosion inside formally democratic regimes. Erosion onset is coded as the first year of a sustained three-year decline of at least 0.03.

The gate and its two paths

A market shock reaches liberal-democratic quality through two terms, and the design measures them separately. The first is arithmetic: the market-minus-disposable Gini gap absorbs part of any shock before the distribution reaches citizens, and this works even if no citizen changes behavior. The second is behavioral: the residue of the shock that does reach citizens, gated by constituency formation, by which a middle class that uses public services holds a material stake in the institutions erosion would capture, and by credible pre-commitment, by which universal automatic programs block the populist-promise equilibrium.

d LibDem / d m = [ ∂LibDem/∂d · (1 − φ(W)) ] fiscal filtering + [ ∂LibDem/∂m | d̄ ] behavioral transmission

A design that treats disposable inequality as the treatment and welfare as the moderator conflates the two terms. This design treats the pre-fisc shock as the treatment and enters the gap directly, as a covariate and as a moderator. If gap control drives the interaction to zero, automatic stabilization is the entire mechanism. If the interaction survives, a behavioral residue exists, and Designs 5 and 6 exist to attribute it.

Estimating the gate

The core specification, Design 1, is a two-way fixed-effects regression with welfare-specific trends:

LibDemit = αi + δt + β1 mi,t−3 + β2(mi,t−3 × Wi) + γ1Wit + γ2Wit2 + X′itθ + εit

The interaction coefficient β2 is the gate. Hypothesis H1 is β2 > 0, with the marginal effect near zero or positive in the high-welfare arm and strongly negative in the thin-welfare arm, reported at the 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th and 90th percentiles of the welfare distribution. The welfare-specific trends are the identification change this registration makes: any factor correlated with welfare strength and with democratic trajectories, EU accession, fiscal capacity, geopolitical alignment, is absorbed in the trend terms, and the gate is identified off deviations from welfare-specific trends. Because two-way fixed effects with differential timing can be biased under heterogeneous effects, the interacted specification is also estimated on five-year differences of the outcome, with Goodman-Bacon decompositions, Callaway-Sant'Anna and Sun-Abraham estimators in the event studies, and wild cluster bootstrap inference for the interaction test.

The six designs

  1. Design 1, the gate test. The two-way fixed-effects specification with welfare-specific trends, plus the gap-controlled variant whose reading is fixed in advance.
  2. Design 2, the adjudication. The inequality main effect with and without the interaction, on the full democracy sample and on the young-democracy sample. A weak main effect with a strong interaction reconciles the two accounts. A surviving unconditional effect favors the materialist account; a null everywhere favors the coalitional account.
  3. Design 3, sequencing. Erosion-onset event studies estimated separately in the high-welfare and thin-welfare arms, with pre-trend tests. The lead-lag structure is the primary defense against reverse causality: services must fall before the index in the thin-welfare arm, and not at all in the high-welfare arm.
  4. Design 4, the dividend. A democratization event study aligned at transition, tracing the top-10 percent share, services and education over two decades by welfare legacy. The dividend window is fixed at years 0 to 7, so it precedes the 2008 crisis and the 2010–2022 resilience window for every retained case.
  5. Design 5, mechanisms. Four arms: gap control for stabilization; composition and usage controls for constituency formation; the automaticity index for pre-commitment; and a proximate-channel report on polarization and educated-youth unemployment.
  6. Design 6, robustness. Seven pre-committed cells, every one reported including the failures: alternative treatments, alternative welfare measures, population weights, commodity-boom exclusion, within-Eastern-Europe estimates and the early-erosion exclusion, wild cluster bootstrap inference, and the placebo moderators.

Why the design is shaped this way

Everything hangs on one logical move: the pre-determined must precede the outcome it explains. Welfare institutions built in the first democratic decade, before crises arrive, gate the inequality-erosion link; welfare generosity purchased later by incumbents is not a substitute, it is part of the erosion playbook. Five exogeneity probes confront the threat that the first democratic decade itself selects welfare depth: balancing regressions of W on first-decade inequality changes and fiscal collapse; legacy-instrument 2SLS using communist-era schooling enrollment and colonial-era public-service coverage; the within-Eastern-Europe estimate, which holds the communist legacy constant; the placebo moderator, which predicts that a pension-heavy total-spending measure does not gate; and the early-erosion exclusion, which removes cases for which W is post-treatment by construction.

The power analysis is calibrated to the same honesty. Minimum detectable effects are simulated on the realized panel at 80 percent power, under outcome serial correlation of 0.7, 0.8 and 0.9 and effective N of 25, 30 and 35. The study is powered to detect a large conditional effect, which is what the theory predicts. A weak gate will go undetected, and the results paper will say so rather than claim a null.

The five hypotheses

H1 · The gate
The within-country effect of a lagged market-inequality increase on the Liberal Democracy Index is negative and large in thin-welfare countries and near zero or positive in strong-welfare countries; the interaction is positive and significant under welfare-specific trend controls.
H2 · Sequencing
Market-inequality surges and public-services retrenchment lead liberal-democracy declines by three to five years in thin-welfare countries, with no anticipatory service decline before erosion onset in high-welfare countries.
H3 · The welfare-dependent dividend
Democratization reduces the top-10 percent income share in the first post-transition years only in high-welfare-legacy countries, and the size of that first-decade decline predicts 2010–2022 resilience.
H4 · Mechanisms
At least one discriminating result: gap control kills the interaction (stabilization); composition and usage controls attenuate it (constituency formation); automatic programs gate and discretionary transfers do not (pre-commitment); or the interaction runs through polarization and tightens where education expands without absorption.
H5 · The universalism contrast
Public health and education services gate; total social spending, pension-heavy and transfer-dominated, and late-bought targeted or chauvinist transfers are placebo moderators that do not gate.

The inequality levels do not line up with the erosion outcomes. Hungary's disposable Gini ran between 0.27 and 0.35 over 2005–2016, with a trough of 0.270 in 2009; Poland's fell from 0.38 in 2004 to 0.31 in 2016. The resilient countries were not the most equal: Estonia's Gini held between 0.31 and 0.35, Latvia's between 0.34 and 0.39. The level correlation is weak in exactly the way Haggard and Kaufman describe, which is why the theory is stated in shocks rather than levels: the gate conditions the transmission of inequality increases, not the standing level of inequality.

South Africa is the sharpest case: the cohort's highest inequality, a Gini near 0.65 at its 2005 peak and still 0.61 in 2010, with only modest liberal-democratic decline, from 0.666 in 2010 to 0.610 in 2022. The paper reads the pattern as delayed or partial transmission, and the Design 3 timing tests are what distinguish that reading from a materialist one.

04Limitations

Five limitations are intrinsic to the design, and the paper states them as such rather than as future work (Section 8).

1.Small N and statistical power

The treated sample is 25 to 35 countries, and the interaction is a difference-in-differences of a difference-in-differences. Power for interaction tests is low in samples of this size, and the clustered standard errors rest on a modest number of clusters. The pre-committed MDES analysis defines what the design can detect before estimation; the study will not detect a weak gate, and the results paper will say so if the confidence intervals cannot exclude one.

2.The welfare measure is a proxy

Public health and education spending as a share of GDP measures fiscal effort, not universality, not quality, and not usage by the middle class. Two countries with identical spending shares can differ in whether public hospitals serve the well-off or only the poor. The proxy bias is conservative: measurement error in the gate variable attenuates the interaction and biases against confirmation.

3.Identification of the gate

The welfare measure is pre-determined relative to erosion onset, but it is not randomly assigned; the first democratic decade is itself a period of democratic politics, and governments that consolidated well also maintained services. The balancing regressions, legacy-instrument 2SLS, within-Eastern-Europe estimate, placebo moderator and early-erosion exclusion each address a specific version of this threat, but none eliminates a third factor, state capacity, geography or geopolitical alignment, that drives both welfare maintenance and democratic resilience. EU accession was itself a welfare-building force, so the gate and the accession channel are partially entangled by construction.

4.Measurement of the outcome and the treatment

The Liberal Democracy Index is expert-coded, and its inter-temporal comparability is contested. Gini comparability is limited by differing underlying surveys and income concepts; PIP and WID harmonization does not remove the issue. The threat to the sequencing tests is asymmetric: a misdated erosion onset could manufacture the confirmation pattern for H2. The three-year sustained-decline coding rule, alternative onset codings, and the requirement that the sequencing pattern differ across welfare arms address it, and a pure measurement artifact would not produce that difference.

5.The placebo subsample problem

The sharpest contrast, H5, requires a full-sample placebo. The OECD SOCX measure exists only for OECD members, so it runs as a secondary subsample with its coverage limitation quantified. If the ILO or IMF full-sample transfer series proves unavailable on OWID for a substantial share of the roster, that finding is itself a registered limitation, and the paper would say so.
