# The Welfare Gate: Pre-Fisc Inequality, Universal Public Services, and Democratic Resilience in Post-1990 Democratizers

> **TL;DR** — A pre-registered research design adjudicating among materialist, coalitional, and institutionally gated accounts of post-1990 democratic erosion. The treatment is pre-fisc market inequality (WID pre-tax top-10 percent income share; market Gini secondary), measured against a pre-determined welfare gate of public health and education spending built in the first democratic decade, with the market-minus-disposable Gini gap directly measuring the automatic-stabilization leg. The gate is identified off welfare-specific trends with staggered event-study estimators built for differential timing; the cohort is capped at 1989-1998 transitions so welfare precedes the 2008 crisis and the 2010-2022 resilience window; a fixed roster, coverage matrix, legacy-instrument 2SLS, exogeneity probes, and a simulation-based minimum-detectable-effect analysis are deposited in a timestamped registry. Confirmation requires a significant pre-fisc-inequality-by-welfare interaction with the marginal-effect pattern in the primary specification and at least 5 of 7 robustness cells; disconfirmation adjudicates toward unconditional materialist or purely coalitional accounts. No models have been estimated: the paper is an honest pre-registration, and a null result is itself the contribution.

<summary>Full hypothesis</summary>

STATEMENT: Among post-1990 democratizers whose transition began by 1998, a rise in inequality — the WID pre-tax national-income top-10 percent income share as the primary treatment, the WID pre-tax national-income Gini as secondary, and the PIP market-income Gini as a same-source robustness wherever OWID carries it — erodes liberal-democratic quality three to five years later only where welfare institutions inherited at transition and maintained through the first post-transition decade are thin. In countries whose early post-transition years universalized public health and education into services the broad middle class actually uses, inequality surges do not transmit into declines in the V-Dem Liberal Democracy Index; in countries where public services stayed thin, the same surges predict graded erosion. The gate is the universal service state, not welfare effort in general: total social spending (pension-heavy, targeted cash, chauvinist transfers) does not gate, and welfare generosity purchased later by incumbents is part of the erosion playbook, not a substitute.

INCOME-CONCEPT CORRECTION, GOVERNING ALL MEASUREMENT: The primary treatment is not market income. WID pre-tax measures are national-income concepts measured after the operation of the pension system and unemployment insurance; they stand conceptually between market and disposable income, and pension-heavy welfare states mechanically compress them. The word "market income" is reserved for the PIP market-income Gini and for the theoretical shock m in the decomposition; every series carries a concept tag, and a concept-mapping note states where the theory's m and the available measures diverge. Because part of the fiscal filtering the gate comprises is already inside the pre-tax national-income treatment, any gap-attribution result is read as an upper bound on the stabilization contribution, and the same-source PIP market-minus-disposable gap, where both series exist, is the closest available measure of true automatic stabilization.

MEASUREMENT: THE TREATMENT IS PRE-FISC TO THE MAXIMUM EXTENT OWID CARRIES, AND THE FILTER IS MEASURED DIRECTLY. The disposable-income Gini is post-treatment with respect to automatic stabilization: high-welfare fiscal systems mechanically compress post-fisc inequality, so a gate interaction estimated on disposable income would load partly as definitional fiscal filtering even if no behavioral gate existed. The claim therefore separates the two paths the gate comprises: (i) automatic stabilization, measured directly as a fiscal filter — primarily the same-source market-minus-disposable Gini gap where PIP carries both series, secondarily the WID pre-tax-national-income-minus-PIP-disposable gap, with concept tags and coverage flags per country-year, and with the gap's coverage, distribution (including the share of negative or near-zero values), and calendar range (expected around 2000 onward) reported and interpreted; and (ii) behavioral transmission, the residual link from the inequality shock citizens experience to democratic quality, gated by constituency formation via policy feedback (the middle class holds a material stake in the public institutions that erosion would capture; Pierson 1993; Soss and Schram 2007; microfoundation in Rothstein and Uslaner 2005) and by credible pre-commitment (automatic, entitlement-based universal programs pre-empt the elite-retrenchment and populist-promise equilibrium of Acemoglu, Egorov and Sonin 2013). Each mechanism has a discriminating implication: the gap (stabilization), the universal-versus-targeted composition of spending and middle-class usage (constituency formation), and program automaticity (pre-commitment). The polarization arm is retained only as a mechanism report, not as the gate's distinctive content, because Rau and Stokes already invoke inequality-induced polarization.

IDENTIFICATION: THE GATE IS IDENTIFIED ON DEVIATIONS FROM WELFARE-SPECIFIC TRENDS. The core specification includes welfare (W) x time and W x time-squared interactions, because any factor correlated with both welfare depth and democratic trajectories (EU accession, fiscal capacity, geopolitical alignment) would otherwise masquerade as a gate. The gate claim survives only if the inequality x W interaction remains significant once those trends, GDP level, region, and EU accession are controlled, with passing pre-trend tests in staggered event studies estimated with Callaway-Sant'Anna and Sun-Abraham estimators given differential treatment timing.

THE PRE-DETERMINED WELFARE MEASURE: A PRE-CRISIS, PRE-ACCESSION SERVICE LEGACY WITH THREE CO-PRIMARY WINDOWS. W is built from public health and public education spending (percent of GDP, WHO and World Bank EdStats series on OWID). Because the WHO health series begins near 2000, for 1989-1993 transitions the earliest window in which both components exist falls in years 9-11 (the EU-accession-negotiation period for Central and Eastern European cases), so a single "first democratic decade" window is not honestly available for most of the roster. Three co-primary measures are pre-committed, each with a concept-composition flag recording which components enter and their actual calendar years: (a) education-only W over the earliest available three-year window within years 0-8 — the component actually observable in the first post-transition decade; (b) the health-plus-education sum over its earliest available three-year window, disclosed and flagged wherever that window falls after year 8; and (c) health-only W over its earliest window. The theory language is correspondingly reframed: the gate variable is the service legacy inherited at transition and maintained through the first post-transition decade, observed in the pre-crisis, pre-accession window that the data actually realize — "built in the first democratic decade" is reserved for cases whose data allow years 0-8 — and the T+3..7 versus T+8..12 sensitivity pair is reported so any window drift is visible. The cohort cap (transition year at most 1998) keeps W before the 2008 crisis and the 2010-2022 resilience window for every retained case. Countries with erosion onset within the first 12 years are excluded or recoded in robustness. W's exogeneity is probed directly: W regressed on first-decade inequality changes and fiscal-collapse indicators (inflation, output collapse); 2SLS with legacy instruments (communist-era schooling enrollment, colonial-era public-service coverage); and a within-Eastern-Europe estimate holding the communist legacy constant, with the post-communist welfare-legacies literature (Cook 2007; Inglot 2008) grounding the inherited-not-chosen claim.

THE WELFARE-DEPENDENT DIVIDEND: SHIFT-ROBUST WINDOWS. The inequality-reduction leg of the democratic dividend (Acemoglu, Naidu, Restrepo and Robinson 2015, stated on top-income shares, which is what that work actually reports) is predicted to materialize only in high-welfare-legacy countries. Because WID pre-tax top-10 percent shares rose during the first post-transition years in the post-communist high-welfare cases as liberalization disequalized, the dividend window is co-primary over three windows — years 0-7, years 3-10, and years 5-12 — with the transition-shock justification for the shifted windows pre-committed. A rise-in-both-arms pattern is pre-specified: it is read as the liberalization-disequalization shock unless the non-interpolated-benchmark robustness and the (supporting) dividend-resilience leg support the dividend-failure reading. The dividend test is restricted to non-interpolated WID benchmark years as a pre-committed robustness, since interpolated 1990s top shares are smoothed and bias the dividend toward zero. The claim that the size of the first-decade dividend predicts 2010-2022 resilience is demoted to a supporting analysis: at an N of roughly 25-30 it cannot carry a meaningful minimum detectable effect, so it is reported with realized power and does not count toward H3 confirmation.

STATUS AND CRITERIA. No model has been estimated; this remains a pre-registered, empirically untested hypothesis, not a finding. The protocol, the fixed country roster with a coverage matrix (country x variable x window; every OWID series URL confirmed or refuted at construction, including the existence of the PIP market-income Gini on OWID, the WID top-10 share for each roster country, the OWID hosting of the Boix-Miller-Rosato cross-check, and the full-sample transfer placebo), and a simulation-based minimum-detectable-effect analysis (N of 25-35 under serial correlation rho 0.7-0.9) are deposited in a timestamped registry in the same iteration as estimation execution, with the registry ID and deposit timestamp printed; the effective estimation sample per design is pre-printed and listwise-deletion consequences below about 25 countries pre-specified. Feasibility is coverage-gated: if the WID top-10 share is missing for more than about 20 percent of retained cases, the pre-tax national-income Gini becomes the primary treatment; if no market-income Gini is OWID-hosted, the same-source gap is unavailable and the filter measure is the pre-tax-minus-disposable gap with concepts tagged; if no full-sample transfer series is OWID-hosted, the universalism contrast falls back to the composition contrast and is reported as a limitation. CONFIRMATION requires: (a) a significant positive inequality x W interaction on liberal-democracy trajectories under welfare-specific trend controls, with the inequality marginal effect near zero or positive at high W and strongly negative at thin W, significant in the primary specification and in at least 5 of 7 pre-specified robustness cells under a pre-committed cell hierarchy (differential-timing estimators, the within-Eastern-Europe estimate, the early-erosion exclusion, and the trend-controlled interaction outrank population weights and placebo cells, so 5 of 7 cannot be satisfied by the weakest cells alone), with failures reported and interpreted; (b) sequencing: inequality surges and public-service retrenchment lead Liberal Democracy Index declines by three to five years in thin-welfare countries only, no anticipatory service decline before erosion onset in the high-welfare arm, and pre-trend tests passing; (c) the welfare-dependent dividend: the top-10 percent share declines relative to its pre-determined path in at least two of the three co-primary windows in high-welfare-legacy countries, with the pre-specified rise-in-both-arms reading and the non-interpolated-benchmark robustness reported; (d) H4 with pre-ranked arms — stabilization is the primary mechanism arm and H4 is confirmed only if its pre-registered pattern obtains (the gap-controlled specification absorbs a pre-committed share of the interaction, reported with the absorbed share's confidence interval and sensitivity bounds on mediator measurement error, not a binary collapse-or-survive rule), constituency formation is secondary, pre-commitment is tertiary, and the proximate-channel arm (polarization, educated-youth unemployment) is a mechanism report that does not count toward H4 confirmation; and (e) the universalism contrast with a full-sample transfer placebo (ILO World Social Protection Database or IMF GFS covering all retained countries), because the OECD-only SOCX subsample cannot carry the sharpest claim of the paper. A leave-one-out influence analysis dropping each of about five influential cases (Russia, Ukraine, South Africa, Hungary, Poland, wherever retained) is pre-committed, because single cases can dominate a 25-35-country interaction. DISCONFIRMATION: a homogeneous inequality effect across welfare arms; an insignificant or wrong-signed interaction; an interaction that collapses once W x time trends are added; reversed sequencing (erosion precedes service decline); a gate reproduced by the pension or targeted-cash placebo; or a dividend that appears in thin-welfare countries. Each outcome falsifies the hypothesis as stated and adjudicates among unconditional materialist, purely coalitional, and outcome-transformation accounts of post-1990 erosion; a null result is itself the contribution.

MOTIVATION: The field holds two contradictory accounts of post-1990 erosion: the materialist (Rau and Stokes 2025 on uninterrupted democracies; Houle 2009 for the coup era) and the coalitional (Haggard and Kaufman 2021 from close case comparison). Neither tests the conditioning structure, and prior global tests were further entangled because disposable-income inequality is partly a product of the very welfare institutions hypothesized to gate, while the pre-tax national-income measures used as structural treatments are compressed by pensions and unemployment insurance, a residual entanglement the design bounds via the same-source gap. The welfare-gate hypothesis resolves the contradiction: inequality matters, but only where welfare institutions are too thin to absorb and convert it; both accounts hold in different institutional environments. Measuring the fiscal filter directly makes the gate test and the stabilization-mechanism test separable, and the corrected income concepts make the separation honest about the treatment's residual post-pension content. For any future democratization wave the policy implication is unchanged and directly actionable: universal health and education systems must be built in the first post-transition decade, before crises and accession negotiations arrive. Everything remains testable on public OWID panels, with one disclosed non-OWID input, the Boix-Miller-Rosato cross-check for transition dating (its OWID hosting verified at coverage-matrix construction; the disclosure is kept either way).

ASSUMPTIONS: (1) The V-Dem Liberal Democracy Index (OWID) is a valid continuous measure of graded democratic quality for young democracies. (2) The WID pre-tax national-income top-10 percent share is a valid treatment that stands between market and disposable income — pre-fisc to the maximum extent OWID carries, with the residual pension/UI transfer content inside the treatment disclosed, concept-tagged, and bounded by the same-source gap robustness; the disposable-income Gini is robustness only. (3) W (education-only, health-plus-education, and health-only co-primary windows) is a pre-determined proxy for universalistic services, not a product of first-decade inequality dynamics or fiscal collapse; actual calendar windows are disclosed and flagged when they fall beyond year 8; probed by exogeneity regressions, 2SLS on legacy instruments, and recoding of early-erosion cases. (4) Post-1990 transitions (1989-1998) are quasi-exogenous in timing and welfare legacies (communist-era health and education systems — Cook 2007; Inglot 2008 — and colonial-era public services) are inherited rather than chosen; every retained case has W measured before erosion onset and before the 2010-2022 resilience window. (5) No unobserved confounder drives both welfare maintenance and democratic resilience; probed by welfare-specific trend controls, within-Eastern-Europe analysis, placebo interactions (e.g., population age structure), and legacy-instrument 2SLS. (6) Cross-country comparability of PIP/WID inequality and WHO/EdStats spending series is adequate for the retained panel; coverage gaps (early-1990s PIP sparsity, WHO health from about 2000, ILO mismatch series availability, non-OECD transfer data) are documented in the coverage matrix, and feasibility is coverage-gated with pre-committed measure fallbacks that bound the effective estimation sample.

INVESTIGATION APPROACH: OWID-only country-year panel, 1985-2023 (main analyses 1990-2022; four pre-transition years support event-study pre-trends); treated sample of post-1990 democratizers with transitions 1989-1998 (about 25-35 countries), plus all democracies for comparison. Six pre-registered designs. Design 1 (gate test): two-way fixed-effects regression of the Liberal Democracy Index on lagged (t-3) pre-fisc inequality, its interaction with pre-determined W, country and year fixed effects, W x t and W x t-squared trends, GDP level, region, and EU-accession controls; marginal effects of inequality at the 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th and 90th percentiles of W with wild-cluster bootstrap; five-year-differences and Goodman-Bacon decomposition as checks; gap-controlled variant with the partial-attenuation reading (share of interaction absorbed, its confidence interval, and sensitivity bounds). Design 2 (adjudication): baseline inequality main effect without interaction, then with interaction, on the full democracy sample and the young-democracy sample. Design 3 (sequencing): staggered erosion-onset event studies by welfare arm with Callaway-Sant'Anna and Sun-Abraham estimators and pre-trend tests, tracing inequality, public services, and the index around onset. Design 4 (democratization event study): dynamic path of the top-10 percent share and public services after transition by welfare legacy; three co-primary dividend windows (0-7, 3-10, 5-12); non-interpolated-benchmark robustness; first-decade dividend size predicting 2010-2022 index change reported as a supporting analysis with realized power. Design 5 (mechanisms): pre-ranked arms — stabilization primary (gap control; absorbed-share CI and sensitivity bounds), constituency formation secondary (spending composition and middle-class usage proxies), pre-commitment tertiary (program automaticity); polarization and ILO educated-youth unemployment reported as a non-confirmatory mechanism arm. Design 6 (robustness): seven ranked cells — trend-controlled interaction, within-Eastern-Europe estimate, early-erosion exclusion, differential-timing estimators, alternative treatments and welfare measures, population weights, wild-cluster bootstrap confidence intervals — plus the full-sample transfer placebo, the population-age-structure placebo, W exogeneity regressions and 2SLS on legacy instruments, commodity-boom exclusion, and the pre-committed leave-one-out influence analysis. Implemented in Python (linearmodels, statsmodels, pyfixest for Sun-Abraham; Callaway-Sant'Anna via documented R/rpy2), clustered standard errors; zero external data beyond OWID plus the disclosed BMR/RoW transition cross-check; no LLM spend.

RELATED WORK: Svolik 2015 (young democracies' distinct vulnerability to incumbent takeover defines the population at risk, but conditions nothing on institutions for the inequality channel, which the gate adds); Huber and Stephens 2001 and 2012 (welfare states underwrite democratic legitimacy and consolidation in Latin America: the closest existing statement of the gate's constituency logic, absent the inequality-shock interaction structure and the post-1990 cohort); Rothstein and Uslaner 2005 (universalism to equality to trust to institutional legitimacy: microfoundation for constituency formation); Rau and Stokes 2025 (unconditional materialist main effect, no moderators, sample excludes the post-1990 cohort, polarization already invoked there); Haggard and Kaufman 2021; Houle 2009; Acemoglu, Naidu, Restrepo and Robinson 2015 (dividend on taxation, schooling, and top-income shares, with imprecise Gini estimates, contrary to how the chapter is sometimes cited); Acemoglu, Egorov and Sonin 2013; Korpi and Palme 1998; Esping-Andersen 1990; Pierson 1993; Soss and Schram 2007; Cook 2007 and Inglot 2008 (post-communist welfare legacies as inherited rather than chosen); Szikra and Oektem 2023, Lendvai-Bainton and Szelewa 2021, Vanhuysse 2006, Benczes and Orzechowska-Waclawska 2024 (outcome side: welfare transformed under backsliding, separated from the gate by the lead-lag tests); Campante and Chor 2012 (absorption channel); Callaway and Sant'Anna 2021, Sun and Abraham 2021, Goodman-Bacon 2021 (staggered difference-in-differences).

INSPIRATION: Three cross-domain transfers structure the hypothesis. (1) Control theory, disturbance rejection: the gain with which an exogenous disturbance propagates to a state variable depends on a feedback controller; the empirical translation is exactly the interaction design this literature has not run. (2) Public-health effect modification: institutions as modifiable protective exposures that gate a socioeconomic risk factor's harm, standard in epidemiology, almost absent from the inequality-democracy literature. (3) Ecology's resistance-versus-resilience distinction: fate of a disturbed system depends on buffering capacity, not the disturbance alone. The revision adds a fourth: the fiscal-filtering insight, in which the entanglement between post-fisc inequality and welfare institutions is turned into the direct, measured test of the stabilization mechanism — now stated on corrected income concepts, because the pre-tax national-income treatment itself contains residual pension and unemployment-insurance filtering that the design discloses and bounds rather than assumes away.

SUMMARY: Rising inequality predicts graded democratic erosion among post-1990 democratizers (transitions 1989-1998) only where universal public health and education services inherited and maintained through the first post-transition decade are thin — measured on corrected income concepts (pre-tax national income, not market income), with the gate's stabilization leg measured directly as the fiscal filter (same-source market-minus-disposable Gini gap, pre-tax-minus-disposable as disclosed fallback), its behavioral legs as constituency formation and credible pre-commitment; the interaction is identified off welfare-specific trends, the dividend windows are shift-robust, the mechanism arms and robustness cells are ranked ex ante, and a leave-one-out analysis guards against single-case dominance.
